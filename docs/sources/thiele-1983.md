## Thiele's own text

- **pp. 17–18.** His first pattern put Hezekiah's fourteenth year at 702 and Samaria's fall at 711.
  Assyrian study "revealed the unexpected evidence that 853 rather than 854 was the correct date for
  Qarqar and that 841 rather than 842 was the true date for the eighteenth year of Shalmaneser III".
  Adjusting Ahab and Jehu gave 701 for Hezekiah's fourteenth year but 710 for Samaria; the
  synchronisms of 2 Kings 15:32 and 16:1 then gave "723/22 for the last year of Hoshea and
  Samaria's fall".
- **pp. 23–24 (Preface to the Third Edition, dated Angwin, California, April 1982).** "my date of
  853 for the battle of Qarqar and 723 for Samaria's fall. The accepted date for Qarqar then was
  854 … the accepted date for the fall of Samaria was 722". 701, Sennacherib against Jerusalem in
  Hezekiah's fourteenth year, "is a major anchor point in my pattern". "precisely 152 years from
  the death of Ahab to the fourteenth year of Hezekiah", matching Assyrian chronology from Qarqar
  853 to 701. Closing paragraph states his purpose: "to bring confidence in the biblical numbers of
  the Hebrew kings, in the God who placed them on their thrones".
- **p. 37 (chapter "The Problems of Old Testament Chronology").** Reign totals Jehu→Hoshea 143 y 7 m
  (Israel) against Athaliah→Hezekiah's 6th 166 y (Judah); 2 Kings 18:10 puts Samaria's fall in
  Hoshea's ninth year = Hezekiah's sixth. "There are only 120 years from 841 B.C., the eighteenth
  year of Shalmaneser III, when the latter reported having received tribute from Jehu—usually
  conceded to be very early in Jehu's reign—to the accession of Sargon in 722/21, when the latter
  claimed to have captured Samaria." Israel's total "about 23 years too high", Judah's "about 46".
- **p. 36 (facing; number not visible).** Reign totals Jeroboam I→Joram 98 y 7 d against
  Rehoboam→Ahaziah 95 y; Omri/Asa synchronism problem (1 Kings 16:23, 28–29).
- **pp. 56–57.** Method: Judah shifted from accession-year to nonaccession-year reckoning and back;
  the Jehoram synchronism of 2 Kings 8:16 first revealed it. 45 vs 47 years Jehu→Jehoash's 2nd.
- **Chapter "The Chronology of Judah and Israel (930–841 B.C.)", opening pages (numbers not
  visible).** "having fixed 931/30 as the year of the division of the monarchy"; 930 used "in the
  interests of simplicity"; 931 "might have been equally appropriate or even more accurate".
  Table of official vs actual reigns Jeroboam I→Ahab: 84 y 7 d official, 78 y 7 d actual. "With an
  interval of 78 years between the accession of Jeroboam I and the death of Ahab, and with the
  latter taking place in 853 B.C., we thus secure the date 931/930 B.C." 701 quoted from the Taylor
  Prism: Sennacherib "shut up 'Hezekiah the Jew … like a caged bird in Jerusalem, his royal
  city'". Charts run "from the disruption of the monarchy in 930 to the release of Jehoiachin from
  prison in Babylon in 561".
- **p. 180.** Babylonian tablets by reign with B.M. numbers: Nabopolassar ac–3 (626–23) B.M. 25127;
  10–17 (616–9) B.M. 21901; 18–20 (608–6) B.M. 22047; 21 (605) B.M. 21946; Nebuchadnezzar ac–11
  (605–594) B.M. 21946; Neriglissar 3 (556) B.M. 25124; Nabonidus ac–17 (555–39) B.M. 35382. Fixed
  dates: Egyptian campaign against Haran 609; Carchemish 605; death of Nabopolassar 16 August 605;
  accession of Nebuchadnezzar 7 September 605; Jerusalem taken 16 March 597; Jehoiachin captured,
  Zedekiah made king 597. Kings and Daniel use Tishri years; Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Haggai, Zechariah
  use Nisan years; Ezra–Nehemiah use Tishri years for Persia (Neh 1:1, 2:1). §36 Josiah 640–609:
  627 Jeremiah's warnings begin (Jer 25:3); 609 Josiah slain at Megiddo (2 Kings 23:29).
- **p. 182.** Diagram 20 (609): Neco at Megiddo in Tammuz; Jehoahaz's three months Tammuz
  (25 June–23 July) to September/October; Jehoiakim's accession Tishri. §38 Jehoiakim 609–598:
  605 Nebuchadnezzar came against Jerusalem in Jehoiakim's third year (Dan 1:1) = fourth year
  (Jer 25:1). Babylonian Chronicle campaigns 608 (Urartu), 607 (Kimuhu), 606.
- **p. 183.** Chart 30 (608–597). Early 605 Egyptians drive Babylonians from Quramati;
  Nabopolassar "stayed at home in his twenty-first year, beginning with Nisan, 605, but sent
  Nebuchadnezzar with the army against Carchemish"; "Nebuchadnezzar conquered the whole area of
  the Hatti-country". Jehoiakim's third year by Tishri reckoning = fourth by Nisan (Jer 46:2).
- **p. 184.** Chart 31 (597–585): 597 Jehoiachin 3 months, Zedekiah 11 years, 8th of
  Nebuchadnezzar Jehoiachin exiled; 593, 31 July, Ezekiel's vision on 4/5/5 (Ezek 1:1–2);
  592, 17 Sept., elders (Ezek 8:1); 591, 14 Aug. (Ezek 20:1–3); 588, 15 Jan., siege of Jerusalem
  begun on 10/10/9 (2 Kings 25:1; Ezek 24:1–2); 587 Zedekiah's 10th = Nebuchadnezzar's 18th,
  Jeremiah in prison (Jer 32:1–2); **586, 19 July, Jerusalem taken on 4/9/11 (2 Kings 25:2–3);
  586, 15 Aug., 19th of Nebuchadnezzar, temple burned on 5/7/19 (2 Kings 25:8–9)**; 585, 8 Jan.,
  exiles hear Jerusalem is taken (Ezek 33:21). "The battle of Carchemish … is now definitely dated
  by Babylonian evidence (B.M. 21946) in the twenty-first year of Nabopolassar, 605 B.C."
- **p. 185.** Tadmor (JNES 15, 1956, p. 227) on Jer 25:1's "first year" as the accession year.
  Nabopolassar died 8 Av (16 Aug.) 605; Nebuchadnezzar took the throne on Elul (7 Sept.), twenty-one
  days later, confirming Berossus via Josephus (Against Apion 1.19.136–39; Antiquities 10.221–24).
- **p. 186.** 604 tribute from Hatti kings incl. Jehoiakim; Askelon captured in Kislev; 601 battle
  with Egypt a "frank acknowledgment of a Babylonian defeat"; 600 king stayed home; 599 desert
  Arabs. §39 Jehoiachin 598–597: 597 taken to Babylon in Nebuchadnezzar's 8th year (2 Kings
  24:12); 561 released in Evil-Merodach's accession year, 37th of exile (2 Kings 25:27). The
  Babylonian tablet: expedition in Kislev (17 Dec 598–15 Jan 597), "captured the city on 2 Adar
  (Saturday, 16 March) 597". Seventh year (Babylonian, Nisan) vs eighth year (Kings, Tishri):
  "The overlap was from Tishri in the fall of 598 to Nisan in the spring of 597. Since Jerusalem
  fell in Adar, the last month of the Babylonian year, this was in the spring of 597." Season of the
  final fall: 2 Chron 36:10 "at the turn of the year" (continues).

## Introduction (by another author; name not on the images)

- **p. 26.** Judges "can be dated only in wide and sweeping generalizations"; Samson's incidents
  "subsequent to the settlement of the Philistines on the coastal plain in the first decade of the
  twelfth century"; "efforts to demonstrate an accurate chronology simply do not merit serious
  consideration". David and Solomon: "we have no chronology, scarcely a scheme of sequences".
  The two kingdoms end "the north in 723/22 and the south in 586 B.C."
- **p. 27.** Menahem's tribute to Pul (2 Kings 15:19) "commonly … 738 B.C., but Professor Thiele
  shows that it must be carried back to 743 or 742". The Septuagint translators "altered the dates
  given in the Hebrew".
- **p. 194.** Prophets, not court scribes, as the authors of Kings; Nathan and Zadok anointed Solomon
  (1 Kings 1:34).
