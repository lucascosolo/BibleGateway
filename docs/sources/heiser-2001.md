## What it gives

- **The witnesses, p. 52.** MT "sons of Israel", also in Aquila, Symmachus and Theodotion (Codex
  X); most Septuagint witnesses "angels of God" (ἀγγέλων θεοῦ), "which is interpretive"; the
  Qumran reading "sons of God" and the minority Greek witnesses follow.
- **The argument, pp. 52–74.** Heiser argues that "sons of God" is the original reading and that
  the verse describes Yahweh's allotment of the nations to lesser divine beings, with Israel kept
  for himself (32:9); he reads this against the divine-council texts (Psalm 82, 1 Kgs 22, Job 1)
  and against Ugaritic material, and answers the objection that this is polytheism.
- Footnote 1 points to Paul Sanders, *The Provenance of Deuteronomy 32* (Leiden, 1996) for the
  wider scholarship on the Song.
