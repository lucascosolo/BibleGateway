# Outside books: source texts and licences (Chitzonim, chunk 1)

Fetched 2026-10-09 for `docs/plans/2026-10-09-outside-books.md` and filed at
`~/.cache/jot/sources/outside/<id>/`: the original download(s) exactly as fetched, the licence
evidence, `text.txt` (the clean extraction) and `meta.json` (every field below, with the full
sha256 of every saved file). The 2026-10-09 researcher list was treated as leads; every licence
below was re-read from the source's own statement, or established by date of first publication
(1930 or earlier, so public domain in the US as of 2026-01-01) together with the translator's death
year (before 1956, so life+70 has also run), with the evidence saved beside the text.

`text.txt` format: UTF-8, NFC, one unit per line as `<ref>\t<text>`, `# ` lines are headers
(`# book: <name>` where a volume holds several works or forms). Refs are the numbering the source
prints; where the source prints none and an ordinal was used, the entry says so. No footnotes,
introductions, headings or page furniture are in `text.txt`.

### `brenton-deutero` — Septuagint extra books (Tobit, Judith, Greek Esther, Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch, Epistle of Jeremy, Susanna, Bel, Greek Daniel, 1-4 Maccabees, 1 Esdras, Prayer of Manasses, Psalm 151)

- **Translator, year:** Sir Lancelot Charles Lee Brenton, 1851 (died 1862; )
- **Edition:** eBible.org eng-Brenton (USFX, generated 2026-10-08 from sources dated 2026-10-07), from the 1870 Bagster diglot English text with documented errata corrections; whole Bible; books TOB JDT ESG WIS SIR BAR LJE SUS BEL 1MA 2MA 1ES MAN 3MA 4MA DAG and PSA 151 extracted
- **Licence:** Public domain (source statement). Evidence: https://ebible.org/find/details.php?id=eng-Brenton; https://ebible.org/eng-Brenton/copyright.htm; copr.htm in the zip: "Published in 1851, and now in the Public Domain."
- **Download:** https://ebible.org/Scriptures/eng-Brenton_usfx.zip
- **sha256:** `eng-Brenton_usfx.zip` 15b551aa90483e74fb16aecc9d78a124ae6709dfbcd325d665ca0264fe316896; `text.txt` 92e659f589a54e3b23ca5cc5098df91d937868b4082d842b191d17a3b66cbd40
- **Numbering as found:** chapter:verse per book; Sirach prologue versified as 1:1, 1:1a.. (subverse letters as in source, 119 lettered subverses overall); Greek Esther 1-10 with the additions integrated; Greek Daniel 1-12 incl. Song of the Three; Psalm 151 as 151:1-7 with superscription inside 151:1 (6041 units)
- **Excluded:** USFX footnotes, cross-references, section headings, the apocrypha preface (OTH), 1844 preface (XXB), appendix (BAK), Jeremiah table (XXA), errata (XXC); protocanonical books
- **Notes:** Source typo "Goliad" in Ps 151:1 kept as found. ESG and DAG contain the canonical Esther and Daniel text too; chunk 3 decides how to address the additions.

### Decisions and cautions for chunks 3-4

- **Aristeas** has three ways forward: proofread a coordinate-based rebuild of the 1917 OCR
  (a start is in `~/.cache/scratch/outside-tools/st-pss-aris/ar.py`); or use H. T. Andrews's
  translation in APOT vol. 2 (1913; Andrews died 1928), which needs its own transcription and
  licence check before it is used; or describe the work without its text. Which one is the
  user's call, because the plan names Thackeray.
- **Editorial ordinals** (`walker-thecla`, the Ethiopic part of `james-apocalypse-peter`) are not
  numbering the source prints. The conventional Lipsius sections for Thecla (1-43) are not in
  ANF. Chunk 4 decides whether to address these by ordinal or to map them to a standard scheme
  with a recorded concordance.
- **Coarse units**: `lightfoot-hermas` units are whole Visions, Mandates and Parables, and the
  Didache/Clement/Barnabas/Ignatius units are whole chapters, because the CCEL transcription drops
  the 1891 verse numbers. A margin note cannot address anything finer until those numbers are
  restored from the scan (archive.org `apostolicfathers00lighuoft`).
- **Brenton ESG and DAG** are the full Greek Esther and Daniel, including the canonical
  portions. The plan's "Additions to Esther" and "additions to Daniel" have to be cut from them
  by verse range in chunk 3.
- **KJV**: eBible's licence page notes the Crown letters patent in the UK, which govern printing
  and importing printed copies, not online display.

## Described works (Qumran and Nag Hammadi): free primary material

These get work pages without a full English text. Pages marked "saved" are filed under
`~/.cache/jot/sources/outside/described-works/` and returned HTTP 200 on 2026-10-09. Link the
others only after re-checking them.

- **Community Rule (1QS)**: the Israel Museum's Digital Dead Sea Scrolls hosts full high-resolution
  photographs with a viewer (http://dss.collections.imj.org.il/community, saved). The Leon Levy
  Dead Sea Scrolls Digital Library has the Cave 4 copies (4Q255-264), for example
  https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/manuscript/4Q258-1.
- **War Scroll (1QM)**: Digital Dead Sea Scrolls, http://dss.collections.imj.org.il/war (saved).
  The Cave 4 War Rule fragments (4Q491-496) are in the Leon Levy library.
- **Thanksgiving Hymns (1QHa)**: held at the Shrine of the Book (https://www.imj.org.il/en/wings/shrine-book)
  and not in the five-scroll digital edition. The Cave 4 Hodayot (4Q427-432) are in the Leon Levy library.
  Hebrew transcriptions of all three are on Qumran-Digital (https://www.qumran-digital.org, saved),
  but its terms reserve copyright, so link to it and do not copy from it.
- **Apocryphon of John (NHC II,1; III,1; IV,1; BG 8502,2), Gospel of Philip (NHC II,3)**: the
  Claremont Colleges Digital Library's Nag Hammadi Archive has facsimile photographs
  (https://ccdl.claremont.edu/digital/collection/nha, saved). The codices themselves are in the
  Coptic Museum, Cairo.
- **Gospel of Mary (BG 8502,1; P.Oxy. 3525; P.Ryl. 463)**: P.Ryl. 463 is at Manchester Digital
  Collections (https://www.digitalcollections.manchester.ac.uk/view/MS-GREEK-P-00463). P.Oxy. 3525
  is in the Oxyrhynchus papyri (https://oxyrhynchus.web.ox.ac.uk/). The Berlin codex is in the
  Ägyptisches Museum, Berlin.
- **Gospel of Judas (Codex Tchacos 3)**: the Coptic edition is National Geographic's and is
  copyrighted. The codex was returned to the Coptic Museum, Cairo.
- **A finding that changes the plan's assumption**: gospels.net publishes Mark M. Mattison's
  translations of the **Gospel of Mary, Gospel of Judas and Gospel of Philip** under the same
  public-domain dedication as his Thomas ("committed to the public domain and may be freely copied
  and used, changed or unchanged, for any purpose"). Each page is saved in `described-works/` as
  `gospels-net-{mary,judas,philip}.html`. These three are not "without free complete translation";
  whether they move from described to printed is a scope decision for the user.
